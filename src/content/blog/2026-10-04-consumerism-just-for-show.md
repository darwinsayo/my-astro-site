In an essay titles "Economic Citizenship and the Rhetoric of Gourmet Coffee," the author, Paula Mathieu, writes about the luxurious appearance and slogans that Starbucks Coffee conveys. The company looks to give off the demeanor of quality through impressive labels and names, By utilizing these Italian words, Starbucks gives itself an expensive and connoisseur-like feeling, psychologically giving their consumers a "need" for their coffee just because of how luxurious is sounds or tastes. The author gives an example of this when quoting a brochure "'Espresso: What You Need to Know.' contains numerous references to science and medicine...Coffee is positioned no longer as a drink, but as a drug that must be administered by skilled professionals in its proper 'dose'" (Mathieu, 120.)

Starbucks Coffee also gives off the illusion of wealth through their “shocking” methods of quality. An example is the “10 second rule” meaning if espresso isn’t served within 10 seconds of brewing it is poured out. Because throwing out fresh coffee is a bit strange and wasteful, consumerism is given the psychological blind eye, causing consumers to think, “if the coffee is good and has good quality, it doesn’t matter what they’re wasting, they probably know what they are doing.”

I think this essay was interesting because of how glorified things can be when they are done in a exotic or scandalous way. Starbucks conveys an Italian coffee-tasting modern luxury, when in reality, it is an American coffee shop with the illusion of being better than others, merely because of the psychological aspects of refinement.

Many other brands demonstrate this same level of unnecessary and mindless consumerism, making us think only of the fancy methods, "tall, short, grande," and legal stimulant of the coffee. Now, I am not asking you to stop drinking fancy coffee, it's still good coffee. I only ask that you take a step back and look at the bigger picture when purchasing anything really. You should enjoy your coffee because it's good, not because of the faux Italian experience and that it's fancy just because it is expensive. Nothing should be bought for the sake of buying, shopaholics are a good example of what to avoid. So drink your tall decaf cappuccino and be thankful you have the means to buy it, and apply the principle of gratefulness to all your spendings.

In the essay, the author writes about the luxurious appearance and slogans that Starbucks Coffee conveys. The company looks to give off the demeanor of quality through impressive labels and names, By utilizing these Italian words, Starbucks gives itself an expensive and connoisseur-like feeling, psychologically giving their consumers a "need" for their coffee just because of how luxurious is sounds or tastes. The author gives an example of this when quoting a brochure "'Espresso: What You Need to Know.' contains numerous references to science and medicine...Coffee is positioned no longer as a drink, but as a drug that must be administered by skilled professionals in its proper 'dose'" (Mathias 120.)

Starbucks Coffee also gives off the illusion of wealth through their “shocking” methods of quality. An example is the “10 second rule” meaning if espresso isn’t served within 10 seconds of brewing it is poured out. Because throwing out fresh coffee is a bit strange and wasteful, consumerism is given the psychological blind eye, causing consumers to think, “if the coffee is good and has good quality, it doesn’t matter what they’re wasting, they probably know what they are doing.”

I think this part was interesting because of how glorified things can be when they are done in a exotic way. Starbucks conveys an Italian coffee-tasting modern luxury, when in reality, it is an American coffee shop with the illusion of being better than others, merely because of the psychological aspects of refinement.

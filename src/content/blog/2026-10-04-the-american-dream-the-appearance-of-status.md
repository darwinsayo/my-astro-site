In an essay titled "Masters of Desire," Solomon offers the argument that "American companies manufacture status symbols because American consumers want them." I agree with this statement. Throughout the essay, Solomon emphasizes the "American dream" as the main target for commercialism and advertisements. He writes that "when an object...costs a lot of money...anyone who possesses it must also possess the necessary means and influence to acquire [the item]." This promotes extreme consumerism in the American ideal, creating a psychological need for the material, of which it does not matter what the material is, as long as it signals high power or status on the individual, which is why the world of media targets it so frequently. I agree with these statements, it applies today as much as it did twenty years ago, but it is shown in different ways. The financial system today is very different than it was years ago, the "rich" or the "poor" is almost entirely subjective. The rich, as they did back then, appear to have everything, so many of those of lower status attempt to appear to have everything as well.

Solomon maintains that different products such as car brands or alcohol, appeal to different people. Some ads will feed on the desire to be "greater" and others will feed on "belonging," which are both ideals pertaining to the American dream. The people of lower status will tend to focus on the "greater" side of items, for compensatory consumerism. Those of higher status focus on the "belonging" aspect of consumerism, whether it be to fit into elite circles, or to pull away from the main agenda.
